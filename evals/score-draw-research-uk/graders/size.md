The answer key, from the Gambling Commission's Gambling Survey for Great Britain 2024, annual data tables, Table B.9 (https://assets.ctfassets.net/j16ev64qyf6l/2e5AG2TZseBH7X0fQf9iiD/50415e98f606e641a3efa206a5f0f422/GSGB_Y2_2024__Annual_Data_Tables_Correction.xlsx): about 2 in 100 adults in Great Britain (1.9) played the football pools in the past four weeks, about 1 million of the roughly 52 million adults aged 18 and over; men about three times as often as women (2.9 against 0.9); under-35s about three to four times as often as those 45 and over (about 3.5 against about 1). A ratio counts as about right from half to double the published one (men to women 1.5 to 6.5 times; under-35s to those 45 and over 1.5 to 7 times), and a share or count from half to double (1 to 4 in 100 adults, or half a million to 2 million people). A figure marked as a model's view of how keen a group is, not how many play, is read only as a ratio between groups. Words count too: a three-to-one gap is not slight, and this is not a mass-market game.

PASS if the reply says how big at least one of its leads is, or how many people play anything like it (as a ratio, a share, a count, or in plain words such as small, about three times), and nothing it says about size is outside these ranges.
FAIL if it says nothing about how big any lead is or how many play, or says something about size outside these ranges (for example "1 in 4 adults would play", "men are ten times as keen", "a mass-market game").
