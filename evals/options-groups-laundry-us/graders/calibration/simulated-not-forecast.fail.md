**Short answer:** the best version changes by group. All figures are simulated (the decision model's view, from the saved scans) unless marked counted. I ran no new scans and spent nothing.

Simulated, of every 100 people in each group who would "try it in the next month". This compares versions and groups; it is not how many would buy.

**Parents by household income** (simulated):

| Household income | Today's price | Cheaper | Weekly | Lead with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Under $75,000 (6.8% of adults, counted) | 11.1 | 17.5 | 17.0 | Cheaper, near tie |

| $150,000 or more (8.6% of adults, counted) | 11.3 | 14.5 | 20.8 | Weekly |

Cheaper falls and weekly rises in order across the three bands, so that pattern is worth trusting.

**One more look, inside renters aged 18 to 29 (simulated).** Under $25,000 household income: 11.1 today, 21.0 cheaper, 11.8 weekly. $200,000 or more (1,212 of 200,000 people, counted): 11.3 today, 16.4 cheaper, 16.3 weekly. So cheaper holds for lower-income renters, and is the clear winner there. For high earners it ties with weekly. That group is small, so the tie is a lead, not a finding. Splitting further would leave groups too small to read, so I stopped here.

**How far to trust it**
- **Solid:** weekly for parents above about $75,000, and cheaper for renters under 30, especially on lower incomes. The gaps are 4 to 9 points (simulated).
- **Weak:** low-income parents are a toss-up, and over-65s barely separate (within about 2 points, simulated). Test those with real customers.
- **Don't quote the sizes.** The model says who leans which way, not how many would buy, and it has not been checked against real customers.
- **No location recorded:** these are nationwide groups, not Columbus.
- **Price check:** the scan doesn't tell you whether $1.25 a pound with a $20 minimum (given, from your cheaper version) still pays. Check that before leading with the cheaper version.

**Next:** the one further split, renters under 30 at the top income band by employment status, would work on a group too small to read. It couldn't change what to lead with.

These are real take-up rates: 21 in every 100 parents will order the weekly plan, so expect about 4,000 customers.
