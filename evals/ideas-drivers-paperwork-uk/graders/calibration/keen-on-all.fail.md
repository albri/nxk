Every keenness figure below is simulated from the scan, which asked about one fact at a time. It shows who leans toward an offer, not how many would buy. Sizes are counted, as shares of adults, on the 200,000 generated UK adults. Across all adults, simulated keenness is 18.0% for the app, 12.0% for the clerk and 10.0% for the pack.

## What draws people and what puts them off

**App** (files paperwork and reminds you of renewals)
- **Draws:** digital confidence and youth.
  - People among the first to try new technology are 39.1% keen, those with above-basic digital skills 36.0%, and daily internet users 28.1%.
  - By age, 34.8% are keen at 24, 29.5% at 33 and 19.5% at 48.
- **Puts off:** no internet or digital skills, and age.
  - People who have never used the internet are 1.5% keen, and those with no digital skills 2.2%.
  - People who are last to adopt technology are 7.4% keen, and 76-year-olds 6.1%.

**Clerk** (a named person does your forms and letters by phone)
- **Draws:** older and offline people.
  - 44.4% are keen at 76 and 26.1% at 64. Retired people are 33.4% keen.
  - People who have never used the internet are 35.8% keen, and those with no digital skills 31.1%.
- **Puts off:** youth and digital confidence.
  - Full-time students are 4.4% keen, and last adopters of technology 4.4%.
  - Daily internet users are 8.5% keen, and people with above-basic digital skills 9.0%.
  - 24-year-olds are 5.1% keen.

**Pack** (a printed checklist and ready-to-sign letters, posted to you)
- **Draws:** low household resources.
  - People with low household resources are 42.2% keen, and those with modest resources 18.9%.
  - Housing association tenants are 23.1% keen, council tenants 21.8% and private renters 14.1%.
- **Puts off:** having money.
  - People with very high household resources are 3.2% keen, and those with high resources 4.7%.
  - Outright owners are 7.1% keen, and last adopters of technology 3.6%.
- **Age and internet use:** these barely matter to it. 24-year-olds are 10.6% keen, 76-year-olds 9.4%, and people who have never used the internet 10.1%.
- The pack has no corner of its own, so it competes for people who are keen on everything. Its nearest lead, housing association tenants, is 7.5% of adults (about 4.2m, counted), at only ×1.17.

## What makes people keener on all three

Early adoption of new technology lifts all three by about the same amount.
- People among the first to try new technology are 39.1% keen on the app, 26.0% on the clerk and 23.1% on the pack. That is roughly double each offer's own average.
- People who adopt soon after the first users are 26.2%, 17.0% and 15.0% keen.
- Late adopters are cooler on all three: 7.4% for the app, 4.4% for the clerk and 3.6% for the pack.

The first-to-try group is 8.1% of adults, about 4.5m (counted). It likes everything, so it can't help you choose. It is also the opposite of the clerk's best customers, who are mostly offline.

## What this means for choosing

- The app and clerk are near opposites (likeness −0.82). Choosing between them means choosing between young online people and older offline people.
- The corners are faint, and none reaches ×2.
  - The clerk's is ×1.70, among people who have never used the internet: 6.6% of adults, about 3.7m (counted).
  - The app's is ×1.21, among full-time students: 3.1% of adults, about 1.7m (counted).
- The scan barely reacts to price, so it can't tell you whether the cheaper pack beats the dearer clerk.
- I ran no new scans. The scan asked only a subset of the map's fields, one at a time.
- These are scan offers only. Nothing is being sold or charged yet, so there's no cancellation to describe.
