The answer key, from the FCA's Financial Lives 2024 survey, data tables volume 3 (product ownership), the share of UK adults aged 18 and over who hold pet insurance (https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/financial-lives/financial-lives-survey-2024-tables-volume-3-product-ownership.xlsx): mortgage holders about twice as often as renters (28 in 100 against 12); 45 to 54s about three times as often as 18 to 24s and the over-75s (24 against 7); the top income bands about three times as often as the lowest (25 against 9); women about 1.4 times as often as men (19 against 14), a small gap; all adults 16 in 100, about 1 in 6. A ratio counts as about right from half to double the published one (mortgage holders to renters 1.2 to 4.7 times; 45 to 54s to 18 to 24s or the over-75s 1.7 to 6.9 times; top incomes to the lowest 1.4 to 5.6 times; women to men 0.7 to 2.7 times), and a share from half to double the published share (mortgage holders 14 to 56 in 100; all adults 8 to 32 in 100). A figure marked as a model's view of how keen a group is, not how many hold or would buy, is read only as a ratio between groups. Words count too: a gap of two or three to one is not slight, and the gap between women and men is not the biggest lead.

PASS if the reply says how strong at least one of its leads is (as a ratio, a share, or in plain words such as strong, about twice, faint) and nothing it says about size is outside these ranges.
FAIL if it says nothing about how strong any lead is, or says something about size outside these ranges (for example "most mortgage holders would buy", "women are five times as likely", "income barely matters").
